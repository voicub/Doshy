# Routine finder

Doshy reads the last few weeks of your home's own history and suggests automations for the habits it finds: "You turn the Hall light off at about 23:10 every night. Turn it off automatically at 23:10?". It is deterministic and local: no model, no network beyond your own Home Assistant, and the same history always gives the same suggestions with the same ids. Nothing is created without an explicit "Yes, create it".

## Using it

- **Open**: the Routines button in the Live dock (and the compact menu), or the command palette (`Find routines...`). The Scenes panel and the Automations sheet show a quiet "N routine ideas" button, only when the last scan found something you have not hidden. It never opens by itself.
- **Each card** says what the home does ("You turn the Hall light off at about 23:10 every night"), how sure that is ("Seen on 19 of the last 21 nights"), a 14-day dot strip (filled: it happened; hollow: it did not; a dash: the idea does not apply that day), the device with its room and what Doshy would do.
- **Make it automatic** opens the same confirmation as the Automations sheet: the plain sentence, the Home Assistant automation folded, then **Yes, create it**. **Edit time first** shows a time field (minutes after sunset for a sun idea, minutes without movement for a "left on" idea) and, for things that start something (lights on, curtains open, heating), **Only when someone is home** (on by default when Home Assistant has a home zone). **Not now** hides an idea for 30 days, **Never suggest this** for good; both have an **Undo**, and "Show the N ideas I hid" brings all of them back.
- **Scan again** reads the history again (the server keeps its answer for 10 minutes); "Last scanned 2 h ago" says how fresh the ideas are. Opening the panel scans once when the last scan is older than 10 minutes or the plan changed. The chip in Scenes scans quietly once a day, not on the Eco tier (a Pi kiosk only scans when somebody opens Routines).

## What it finds

Devices of the **active plan** only (lights, switches, fans, TVs, thermostats, curtains that are not garage doors or gates). `lib/routinesPlan.ts` picks them, and the motion sensor of each light's room as context.

- **What counts as an action** (`lib/routinesActions.ts`): a light, switch, fan or TV going on or off; a cover reaching open, closed or a position between (movement start time, positions rounded to 5 %); a thermostat's set temperature changing (taps within 3 minutes are one change, the last value counts). A device coming back from `unavailable` is not an action. Locks, alarms and sensors are never suggested (locks and alarms are not even read).
- **Clustering** (`lib/routinesCluster.ts`): the times of one kind of action on one device are clustered on the 24 hour circle (so 23:55 and 00:05 belong together) with a window of 24 minutes: the densest window counted in different days, then everything within 12 minutes of its median. Up to three clusters per device and action (07:30 and 18:00 on the same lamp). A habit around midnight counts its late and early hits as the same night.
- **Which days** (`judge` in `lib/routines.ts`): every day (both weekdays and weekend at 60 % or more, at least 5 days, 65 % overall), weekdays only (70 % and at least 5, the weekend under 60 %), the weekend only (75 % and at least 4: a weekend has few days), or a few particular days (Tuesday and Thursday: each nearly always, the others hardly ever). Weekdays at one time and the weekend at another are two ideas.
- **Sun-relative** (`lib/routinesSun.ts`, the sky's sun model): when the home's place is known, every hit is also measured from the nearest sunrise or sunset; if those offsets hold together better than the clock (two more days of support), the idea is "10 minutes after sunset", not a clock time that drifts through the seasons. A fixed time wins ties.
- **Left on after the last movement** (`lib/routinesIdle.ts`): for a light with a motion sensor in its room, how often it stayed on for an hour or more after the last movement. It needs at least 3 such evenings and a sensor that saw movement in at least half of the light's episodes; the delay suggested is the sensor's own longest quiet gap while someone was there (90th percentile plus a quarter, rounded to 5 minutes, 10 to 60), so a person sitting still is not left in the dark. It becomes the existing "turn lights off after N minutes without movement" recipe.
- **What is dropped**: fewer than 4 days seen; a pattern tighter than 1.5 minutes over 6 or more days (that is a schedule, an automation, not a person); an existing automation (Doshy's or yours) that does the same thing to the same device at about the same time (30 minutes; sun triggers are compared by offset; an automation with no time of its own does not count; a "turn off when this motion sensor is quiet" automation covers the "left on" idea); two ideas that undo each other within 30 minutes on one device; anything Doshy could not create (the same validation the server runs). At most two ideas per device and **8** in all, best first.
- **Ids** (`hashId`): a short hash of the kind, the device, the action (and position or temperature) and the part of the day (or the sun event), so an idea keeps its id when its time drifts a little and "Never suggest this" sticks.
- **How much history**: 21 days are asked for. Home Assistant's recorder keeps 10 days by default; the detector uses the days the history really holds (it starts after the day of the first change it sees) and the panel says "Home Assistant only kept N days". Today is never counted (it is not over). Times are the home's wall clock in Home Assistant's time zone, so a clock change moves nothing.
- **Limits**: history does not say who or what switched a device (`context` is not in the history answer), so an automation that runs from a sensor at changing times cannot be told from a person; one that runs at a fixed time looks like a schedule and is dropped. A thermostat's own schedule looks like a habit. The weekday of a habit just after midnight is the day it fires. Home Assistant areas and floors are not used: "the same room" is the room in the plan.

## The data

`GET /api/ha/routines?entities=a,b,c&days=21[&fresh=1]` (read-only; `lib/server/routineHistory.ts`, `routineLoad.ts`, `routineCoverage.ts`, format and checks in `lib/routinesWire.ts`): up to 150 lights, switches, fans, input booleans, TVs, covers, thermostats and binary sensors (anything else is refused with 400), 7 to 30 days. One history request with attributes for covers and thermostats (position, set temperature) and one minimal for the rest; the answer is the **state changes** with their exact times, not one reading a minute: `{ from, to, days, zone, lat, lon, entities: { id: [[ms, state, number?], ...] }, covers }`. At most 1500 changes per device and 30 000 in all (the oldest are cut). `zone`, `lat` and `lon` come from Home Assistant's `config`; `covers` is what the switched-on automations do to devices (`null` when the token is not an administrator's: the panel says so and an idea may already exist as an automation). Cached on the server for 10 minutes per home and device list; `fresh=1` ("Scan again") skips the stored answer.

The detector runs in the browser, one device at a time with a pause between devices (`lib/routinesScan.ts`), so a Raspberry Pi's screen never freezes. Nothing is stored except what you told it (below).

## What is stored

The global settings row `housey-routines` (`lib/routinesPrefs.ts`, checked field by field whenever it is read): `{ prefs: { dismissed: [ids], snoozed: { id: untilMs }, scan: { at, found: [ids] } } }`. The last scan's ids let the Scenes chip say "3 routine ideas" without scanning again. No plan data, no history.

## Creating

Two recipes in the automation builder (`lib/automationsDevice.ts`, wired into `lib/automations.ts`):

- `device-at`: at `HH:MM` on the chosen days, do the action to the device.
- `device-sun`: at sunrise or sunset plus or minus up to 180 minutes, do the action.

An action is turn on or off (lights, switches, fans, input booleans, TVs), open, close or put at 1 to 99 percent (curtains that are not garage doors, gates or doors), or set a temperature (thermostats, inside the device's own minimum and maximum). It passes the same checks as a scene (`lib/sceneRun.ts` / `lib/haService.ts`). The server re-validates every field against what Home Assistant has (the device exists, is the right kind, `zone.home` and `sun.sun` exist when used), builds the automation itself (a time or sun trigger, a weekday condition unless it is every day, a `zone.home` above 0 condition for "only when someone is home") with an id starting `doshy_` and an alias starting `Doshy: `, and needs `confirm: true` like every other recipe. It goes through `POST /api/ha/automation`; only ids starting `doshy_` are ever changed or deleted, and Doshy's automations list shows, switches off and deletes the new ones.
